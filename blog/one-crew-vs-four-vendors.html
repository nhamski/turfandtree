One Crew vs. Four Vendors: What Juggling Lawn, Irrigation & Landscaping Really Costs You

Most properties around Logan end up with a mower guy, a sprinkler guy, a landscaper, and a nursery three towns over — four phone numbers, four invoices, and nobody who actually owns the whole yard. The invoices are the visible cost. The real cost is everything that happens between them.

The coordination tax nobody bills you for

When the sprinkler guy fixes a leak but doesn't tell the mower guy where he dug, you get a scalped strip of grass over the repair the next week. When the landscaper puts in a new bed where a zone used to run full-coverage, the irrigation schedule doesn't automatically adjust — someone has to notice, and usually that someone is you. None of these show up as a line item. They show up as your time, your attention, and a yard that never quite looks like the sum of what you're paying for.

Scheduling around four calendars instead of one

Four vendors means four separate scheduling conversations, four sets of availability, and four different definitions of "we'll get to it this week." If your landscaper needs the sprinklers off to work a bed, that's a conversation you're relaying, not one they're having directly. One crew means one call moves the whole job — the person doing the landscaping already knows the irrigation layout because they're the same outfit that installed it.

Diagnosis gets skipped, not solved

A generalist mowing crew will mow around a dead patch. Diagnosing why it's dead — a bad head, compacted soil, a controller zone that's stopped firing — takes someone who actually works on irrigation, not just grass. When those are two different companies, the mowing crew has no reason to flag it, and the irrigation company isn't there often enough to notice. The problem sits until it's bad enough that you notice it yourself.

The real math

Compare what you're actually paying: four separate service minimums, four separate trip charges if something needs a second look, and the hours you spend being the project manager between them. A single crew that handles lawn care, irrigation, hydroseeding, and landscaping doesn't necessarily charge less per task — but it eliminates the trip-charge stacking, the relay conversations, and the gaps where nobody's watching the whole property. For most yards, that adds up to a meaningfully lower total cost than the sum of four separate invoices, even before counting your own time.

When four vendors still makes sense

If you've got a highly specialized need — a certified arborist for a heritage tree, a pool-adjacent irrigation system that needs specific licensing — a specialist beats a generalist every time. The one-crew case is strongest for the routine stuff: mowing, standard irrigation repair, hydroseeding, and everyday landscaping, which is most of what most properties actually need most of the year.
